Black Friday and Cyber Monday are over, and the numbers aren't where you wanted them. Maybe revenue came in under target. Maybe revenue looked fine, but the profit didn't.

It's tempting to switch everything off, blame Meta or promise yourself a bigger discount next year. Don't do any of those yet. A missed target is information. Here's how to use it.

## Step 1: Check the numbers properly

Before you decide anything, make sure you're looking at the real picture.

- **Compare your store data with Meta's.** Meta's reported results usually look better than reality over BFCM, because some of those sales would have happened anyway. Start with what actually landed in Shopify or WooCommerce.
- **Work out your MER.** Total store revenue ÷ total ad spend, across every channel, for the sale period. This is your most honest single number.
- **Check profit, not just revenue.** Revenue minus product cost, shipping, payment fees and ad spend. A sale that hit its revenue target with a 40% discount may have lost money.
- **Split new and returning customers.** Did you find new buyers, or mostly give discounts to people who would have bought anyway?
- **Compare against break-even, not just the target.** If your target was ambitious but you still sold above break-even ROAS, the campaign may have worked, just not as well as hoped.

If you haven't worked out your break-even ROAS yet, the [BFCM playbook](/bfcm/playbook#2-work-out-your-numbers-first) shows how.

## Step 2: Find where it broke

Most disappointing sales fail at one clear point. Look at the numbers in order and find the first place things drop off.

| What you see | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| High cost per 1,000 views (CPM), few clicks | Ads didn't stand out in a crowded feed | Hooks, first frame, how clearly the offer was shown |
| Good clicks, low add-to-cart | The page didn't match the ad, or the offer wasn't compelling | Landing page, price, offer wording, page speed on mobile |
| Good add-to-cart, low checkout | Friction at the last step | Shipping costs shown late, payment options, surprise fees |
| Sales came mostly from existing customers | Not enough new people reached | Warm-up audiences built too late, prospecting budget too low |
| Meta and store numbers very different | Tracking problems | Pixel, Conversions API, purchase values, attribution window |
| Strong first day, weak after | Creative fatigue or small warm audience | Frequency, number of ad variations, audience size |
| Ran out of hero products | Stock planning | Inventory levels against forecast demand |

Write down the one or two biggest causes. Fixing those matters far more than tweaking everything else.

## Step 3: Save what you can in December

The season isn't over. December is still one of the strongest months of the year for many Australian brands.

- **Switch to gifting.** Move from "sale" messaging to "the gift they'll actually use". Gift guides and price-point ads ("gifts under $50") work well.
- **Use honest shipping deadlines.** Your carrier's Christmas cut-off dates are a real reason to buy now. Only use them if they're true for your store.
- **Retarget BFCM visitors who didn't buy.** People who visited, watched or added to cart over the sale weekend are your warmest audience in December.
- **Follow up by email and SMS.** Abandoned carts and browse abandonment from the sale period are worth a second message.
- **Be careful with "extended" sales.** Under Australian Consumer Law, if you said the sale ended on Monday, it needs to have actually ended, or you need to clearly and honestly relaunch it. Don't mislead people who bought early.

## Step 4: Turn Black Friday buyers into repeat customers

The customers you did win are worth more than their first order.

- Send a proper **welcome flow**: how to use the product, what to expect, and how to get help.
- Plan a **second-purchase offer** for January, timed around when they're likely to run out or want something new.
- Ask for **reviews**. You'll need fresh social proof for next year's ads.

A Black Friday customer who buys again in February turns a thin-margin sale into a profitable one.

## Step 5: Write a one-page post-mortem

While it's fresh, answer five questions:

1. What was our real result: revenue, profit, MER and new customers?
2. Where did the funnel break first?
3. What worked that we should do again?
4. What will we change next year? Timing, offer, creative, stock or tracking?
5. When will we start? For most brands, the honest answer is "earlier": audience building in October makes the sale itself cheaper.

Put the answers somewhere you'll see them next September.

## The takeaway

A missed BFCM target usually comes down to one or two fixable problems, not a broken business. Check the real numbers, find the break point, make the most of December and plan next year early.
